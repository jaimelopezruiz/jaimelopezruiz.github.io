Congestion-knee
detection

Past a certain speed, latency climbs steeply and sending faster no longer helps. That point is the knee. I compared two ways of finding it in latency-versus-speed curves: a rule-based detector on smoothed signals, and Gaussian-process regression.

01Same data, two verdicts

Both detectors find a bend in dataset 1. The rule-based one then rejects it on its steepness check, while the Gaussian-process one accepts it.

02Data

A generator stands in for router telemetry: clear knees, soft or ambiguous transitions, gentle rises with no knee at all, and noisy measurements. Because it is synthetic, each detector can be checked against a known answer.

03Detectors
Rule-basedGaussian process
SignalsExponentially weighted averages run low-to-high and high-to-low, their difference, and a noise-change signalA smoothed mean with a 95% band, its slope and its curvature
DecisionThresholds on the difference, gradient and noise, and a minimum speedA curvature threshold, a post-knee slope clearly steeper than before, and a minimum speed
StrengthFast, and every decision is readable on the plotSmooths the noise and shows its own uncertainty
WeaknessSensitive to its thresholdsStill ends in hand-set rules
04What I took from it
  1. Finding a knee and deciding to act on it are separate steps. A knee low in the speed range may not justify capping the link.
  2. The diagnostic plots are worth keeping. With only the verdict, either method looks equally confident.
  3. The data is synthetic and the thresholds are hand-tuned. This is an exploration of the idea and has no feedback controller.
